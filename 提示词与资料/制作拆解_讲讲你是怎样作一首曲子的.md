## Overview

The film is a 4 min 52 s answer to one question, "讲讲你是怎样作一首曲子的": instead of explaining, the AI writes the piece the viewer is hearing, and the picture shows that piece being built note by note, then growing into a 3D landscape and folding back into the project.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Running time | 292.4 s (85 bars plus a free intro and tail) |
| Picture | 1920×1080, 30 fps, 8,773 frames |
| Music | Original score in D major, lifting to E major at bar 57; base tempo 80 BPM with a 60→44 BPM free intro and a 74→50 BPM final ritardando |
| Ensemble | Piano, cello, double bass, drum kit, string section and violin, horn, flute, clarinet, glockenspiel, harp, choir, timpani |
| Language | All on-screen text in Chinese (55 caption cues); code keeps its own syntax |
| Guide character | The original pixel-art Claude desktop pet, unmodified, split into one collaborator per track |
| Visual identity | Near-black background, warm coral accent (RGB 217,119,87), a colour per voice, thin luminous lines |

**How it is built.** Everything comes from code and from one source of truth:

1. `score.py` writes the whole score as data: notes, tempo map, chords and expression curves.
2. `render.c` and `mix.py` play that score through the GeneralUser GS SoundFont and mix the result; the chat typing and send sounds are mixed in too.
3. The same note list is exported to the renderer (`vis5.json`), so every note on screen sits exactly where it sounds.
4. `video.html` draws each frame as a deterministic function `renderAt(t)`, in 2D canvas with a WebGL bloom pass; `journey.js` adds the 3D multitrack journey and `world3d.js` the 3D landscape.
5. Playwright drives Chrome frame by frame; ffmpeg encodes the frames and adds the audio.

Code fragments may transition into notes, tracks, or lines within the scene to create natural visual continuity.
Keep code displays concise, credible, and meaningful. Avoid meaningless scrolling characters used merely to suggest technology. Help the audience understand how code participates in the creative process while preserving the music’s emotion and the visuals’ beauty.
The final goal is to create a music piece that is enjoyable in its own right, while allowing the audience to naturally understand—through listening and watching—how AI uses code to compose the piece and build the visual world that grows alongside it.

## Scene descriptions

Every scene follows the same rule from Brief B: intention, then action, then a visible and audible result. A code card shows one instruction, the pet runs it, the music or the picture changes, and the card clears.

### 1. The question (0:00–0:08)

A chat window on a near-black page. The question is typed through a Chinese input-method animation: pinyin candidates resolve into 讲讲 / 你是 / 怎样 / 作一首 / 曲子的, each with a key-click in the mix (typing starts at 0.55 s, sent at 3.2 s). The pet appears as the reply bubble and says "收到。", then "但是，与其给你讲解原理，不如直接写一首曲子。"

### 2. No one at the piano (0:08–0:25.6, bars 1–4)

The chat dissolves into an empty workspace with a fine timeline. Three short code cards drive the scene:

| Time | Code on screen | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:07.6 | `sketch(Grand(keys=88, lid=35))` | A plotting pen draws a grand piano and an empty bench, which fade |
| 0:11.8 | `sketch(Ensemble(players=3, drums=1, mics=2))` | Outlines of players, a drum kit and two microphones, which recede |
| 0:15.9–0:23.6 | `seed = ["A4", "D5", "E5", "F#5"]`, `rhythm = [0.5, 0.5, 1, 1]`, `melody.add(pitch="A4", beats=0.5)` | The pitch and duration values light up; a ghost note leaves the code and the pet writes it onto the melody track |

The music here is one sustained note over a free, slowing tempo (60→44 BPM), so the captions have room to breathe without the music being stretched.

## Animation, camera and transitions

The film is one continuous shot with no hard cuts: every change of space is a camera move or a transformation, and each one starts on a phrase boundary from the score.

### Camera

| Part | How it moves | Timing rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2D workspace (0:00–2:25) | 26 keyframes of scale and centre, eased in and out (cubic); 2–3 s moves, then holds | Moves start about 2 s before the phrase they frame; holds cover the phrase |

| Ending (4:23–4:34) | Close-up to scale 1.75 on the melody, then a pull-back to scale 1/6 so the project becomes a card in the chat | Pull-back starts as the export bar finishes |

- **World → tracks**: the same match in reverse; the landscape returns along its own paths, the camera arrives top-down on the staff (4:11.6) and pulls out of scale 3 to the full project (4:15.6).

The outward path and the return use the same match point on the harmony staff, so the loop closes where it opened.

### Animation principles

- **One relationship at a time**: each code card holds one instruction; values light up, a ghost of the result leaves the card, then the card fades (0.35 s in, 0.45 s out).
- **The pet stays the original**: pixel map copied from the supplied screenshot; it acts through position, facing and its existing states only. It splits into one collaborator per track and rides a raft through the world.
- **Notes live on the score**: a note lights when its sound starts and stays lit for its length; in the journey, notes lift with their loudness and settle over about 1 s.
- **Identity by colour**: each voice keeps its colour from track to landscape (melody coral, harmony sand, bass deep red, drums ivory, second melody teal, strings rose, winds amber, colour instruments blue).
- **Light**: a WebGL bloom on bright notes, a subtle lens fringe, vignette and film grain; bloom follows the music's loudness.

### Sync method

1. `score.py` holds a tempo map of linear ramps; every note's start and end time is integrated from it, so ritardandos and accelerandos are exact.
2. The same note list, bar times and beat times are written to `vis5.json` (renderer data) and to a Standard MIDI file.
3. Every visual timing is a bar or beat time plus a deliberate offset: track lanes open 2 s before their instrument enters, code cards appear about 0.6 s before they run, and camera moves start about 2 s before the phrase they frame.
4. A note lights on the frame of its own start time; at 30 fps any visual event is at most one frame (33 ms) after its sound.
5. The opening's typing clicks and send sound are placed in `mix.py` at the same times the chat animation uses (typing from 0.55 s, sent at 3.2 s).

### Sound sources

The notes are composed in code, and the instrument sounds come from one free sample library: the GeneralUser GS SoundFont (v2.0.3, by S. Christian Collins; free for private and commercial music). `render.c`, built on the TinySoundFont library (MIT licence), plays each of the 16 channels through that SoundFont into its own stem. `mix.py` then applies per-track gain, pan and filtering, a synthetic hall reverb, the typing and send clicks, bus compression and a peak limiter. No recordings of existing music are used, and re-running the chain reproduces the final audio bit for bit.

## Rendering and delivery

A full render on your own GPU takes about 13 minutes; the same render on the cloud machine (no GPU, 2 CPU cores, software WebGL) took hours and had to be split into resumable chunks.

| Stage | Tool | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frame drawing | Chrome via Playwright | `renderAt(t)` per frame; 2D canvas for the workspace, WebGL2 for the 3D world and for bloom, lens and grain |
| Capture | Playwright screenshots | JPEG frames piped straight into ffmpeg, no frames written to disk |
| Chunking | `render_local.py` | 300-frame chunks; finished chunks are kept and skipped on the next run; chunks made with older scene files are moved aside automatically |
| Encoding | ffmpeg, x264 | Standard version: two-pass 600 kbit/s (about 27 MB); high-quality version: CRF 18 |
| Fonts | Subset Noto CJK (serif, sans, mono), DejaVu Sans Mono, Lora italic | Bundled with the project so every machine renders the same text |
| Audio | FLAC | Shipped in parts and joined on first run |

**Local render package** (in `D:\aivideo\music\render_project`): `video.html`, `journey.js`, `world3d.js`, `vis5_data.js`, `fonts/`, `song_part_00–02.bin`, `render_local.py`, `开始渲染.bat` and `说明.txt`. The launcher checks for Python and ffmpeg, installs Playwright if needed, reports which GPU Chrome is using, and resumes from the last finished chunk.

**Large-file transfer**: files above about 27 MB timed out when copied to your computer, so finished videos were split into 9 MB pieces with a `.bat` that joins them (`copy /b`).

## Problems, fixes and lessons

Most late-stage problems were motion problems in the 3D world, not design problems; each was traced to a measurable cause before it was fixed.

| Problem (latest first) | Root cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lake surface jittered side to side on your GPU | The water was one huge 160,000-unit triangle pair; per-vertex interpolation lost precision across it, and the reflection was distorted by up to 130 px | Each pixel now computes its own point on the water plane from the camera ray (with exact depth); reflection distortion cut to 0.012 with a light blur; mirrored lines dimmed to 35% |
| Return from the world to the tracks felt abrupt | The return camera followed the river path, so its heading stalled and then spun | A world-space glide with one smooth turn, roll held level, a 2.1 s hand-over; worst rotation jerk fell from 0.067 to 0.019 |
| Raft and boat jumped | Two placement rules switched at a threshold (height 200, time 205 s) | One continuous formula; sideways drift eased in smoothly |
| Bottom of frame shimmered during the fly-through | Water ripples about 13 units apart while the camera moved about 7.6 units per frame (temporal aliasing) | Wider ripple spacing, ripples fade near the camera, hatching removed, calmer surface near the lens |
| Camera never reached the lake | The river course was longer than planned | Course scaled to 62% and the camera re-keyed |
| Seams and dark patches on flat land | A lowered band cut a trench; flat-land normals were wrong | Band limited and blended; normals blended by terrain growth |
| Cloud renders stopped part-way | Background jobs end after 2 hours | Resumable chunk scripts; local GPU render as the main path |
| Video copy to your computer timed out | Files around 27 MB exceeded the transfer limit | 9 MB pieces plus a join script |

### Lessons for the next film

- Keep one data source for sound and picture; it removes sync as a class of problem.
- In moving 3D, spacing between repeated lines must be large compared with how far the camera moves in one frame, or it shimmers.
- Never draw huge surfaces with per-vertex values; compute them per pixel.
- Give the camera its own smooth path for big moves instead of tying it to an object's path.
- Check motion on the target GPU early: software rendering hid the water jitter that your GPU showed.
